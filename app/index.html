20 Questions Before Marriage - Full Guide

Marriage is more than love. It is a lifetime partnership of finances, family, faith and daily habits. Before you say I do, you need clarity. These 20 questions are used by relationship coaches to help couples avoid divorce and build trust before marriage.

1. Why do you want to get married?

Be honest. Is it love, companionship, age pressure, family pressure, or fear of being alone? A marriage built on timing rarely lasts. A marriage built on clarity and commitment survives hard seasons. Discuss your true reason.

2. How do you handle conflict?

Do you shout, go silent, leave the house, or talk calmly? Your conflict style today will be your conflict style in 10 years. Learn how to apologize, how long you need to cool off, and how you reconnect.

3. What about finances?

Will you have joint account, separate accounts, or both? How much debt do you have? What about savings, supporting parents, and spending habits? Money is the top reason couples fight. Agree now.

4. Do you want children and when?

This must be 100% aligned. Not we will see later. Discuss number of kids, when to start, parenting style, discipline, and what if you cannot have kids naturally. This question saves many marriages.

5. Where will you live?

City or village, near your family or far, rent or build, abroad or Nigeria? Location affects job, happiness, support system and cost of living. Don't assume you agree. Decide together.

6. What are your career goals?

Will one person sacrifice career for family? How do you support promotions, business risks, or relocations? A supportive partner celebrates growth instead of feeling threatened by it.

7. What is your love language?

Some people need words of affirmation, some need physical touch, acts of service, quality time, or gifts. If you don't speak each other's language, you will feel unloved even when love exists.

8. How do you deal with in-laws and extended family?

Boundaries are critical. How often will family visit, who makes decisions in your home, how do you handle mother-in-law advice? Discuss this early to avoid third-party interference.

9. What are your views on religion and faith?

Will you attend same church or mosque? How will you raise children? What about prayer, fasting, tithes? Faith differences cause deep conflict if not discussed with respect.

10. What are your non-negotiables?

Cheating, physical abuse, emotional abuse, lying, addiction, porn, gambling — what ends the marriage for you? Say it clearly now, not after it happens. Boundaries create safety.

11. What is your health history?

Chronic illness, mental health, past trauma, genotype, blood group, and sexual health are not secrets to hide. Love needs truth to make informed decisions.

12. What did you learn from past relationships?

Past is not to judge, but to understand patterns. What went wrong before and what will you do differently? Healing before marriage prevents baggage.

13. What are your intimacy expectations?

Needs, boundaries, frequency, and how you handle rejection. Open honest conversation prevents cheating and resentment. Intimacy is emotional and physical.

14. Who does what at home?

Cooking, cleaning, laundry, bills, childcare. Roles based on ability and time, not just gender. A fair home is a peaceful home.

15. What about friends and social life?

How much time with friends, opposite-sex friendships, partying, social media? Social boundaries prevent jealousy and mistrust.

16. Is there anything you have not told me?

Secrets become bombs after marriage. Debts, child outside, criminal record, addiction. One honest talk now is better than 10 years of lies.

17. How will you keep love alive?

Love fades without effort. Date nights, vacations, learning new things together, checking in emotionally. How will you grow, not just survive?

18. How do you handle failure?

What if business fails, job lost, sickness comes? Will you blame or support? Marriage is tested in failure, not success.

19. How do you forgive?

How long do you stay angry? Do you bring up past mistakes? Forgiveness is a skill. Learn it before marriage, not after hurt.

20. Where do you see us in 10 years?

Vision brings unity. House, career, children, ministry, travel. If your 10-year visions don't align, your daily decisions will always clash.
